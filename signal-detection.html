Signal detection: ROR & PRR

PharmaSafe uses disproportionality screening to identify drug–reaction combinations that appear more often in the reporting database than a chosen background comparison would suggest. These statistics are screening tools, not diagnoses.

The 2×2 idea

For a drug–reaction pair, the basic contingency table separates reports that contain both the drug and reaction from reports containing the drug without the reaction, reports containing the reaction without the drug, and reports containing neither. From those four cells, disproportionality measures can be calculated.

ROR

The reporting odds ratio compares the odds of the reaction among reports mentioning the drug with the corresponding odds in the comparison set. PharmaSafe displays the estimate together with a 95% confidence interval so the result is not reduced to one number.

PRR

The proportional reporting ratio compares the proportion of reports for the drug that contain the reaction with the proportion for the broader reporting background. It is another way to screen for disproportionate reporting.

How to read the result

  1. Check the report counts supporting the calculation.
  2. Look at the confidence interval rather than only the point estimate.
  3. Consider whether the underlying reports could be affected by reporting bias, co-medication, indication, publicity, or changes in reporting practices.
  4. Use a signal as a prompt for further investigation, not as evidence that a medicine caused an event.

What a signal does not mean

A statistically disproportionate reporting pattern does not tell you the incidence of the event, the absolute probability of harm, or whether the drug caused the event in a particular patient. The FDA notes that adverse-event reports are not necessarily verified for cause and effect.

Why confidence intervals are shown

Two estimates can look similar while having very different uncertainty. Showing a confidence interval makes that uncertainty visible and helps readers avoid treating a point estimate as a precise measurement.
