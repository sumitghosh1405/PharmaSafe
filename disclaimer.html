SAFETY & LIMITATIONS

Important disclaimer

PharmaSafe is a research and educational tool. Its outputs should be interpreted as information for pharmacovigilance research, not as a diagnosis, treatment recommendation, or proof that a product caused an event.

Spontaneous reports are not proof of causality

FDA and openFDA documentation explain that adverse-event reports are submitted observations and are not necessarily scientifically verified for a cause-and-effect relationship. A report can contain several products and several reactions, and the database does not automatically establish a specific product–reaction causal link.

Counts are not incidence rates

A larger number of reports can reflect greater use, reporting behavior, publicity, product age, or other factors. Without an appropriate denominator and study design, a spontaneous-report count should not be read as the probability that a patient will experience an event.

Not for individual medical decisions

Do not use PharmaSafe results to start, stop, change, or substitute medication or treatment. For a personal health decision, consult a licensed healthcare professional and review current authoritative product information.

Data can change

Public datasets are updated and revised. PharmaSafe reports what its source returns at query time; it does not guarantee that a result is complete, permanent, or identical to a later FDA release.

Research interpretation

Signal-screening statistics are hypothesis-generating tools. A finding should be corroborated using additional evidence, such as product labeling, clinical literature, case review, epidemiologic analysis, or regulatory assessment as appropriate.

Primary references: openFDA Drug Adverse Event documentation and FDA AEMS overview.
